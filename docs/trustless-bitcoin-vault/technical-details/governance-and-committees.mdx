# Governance and committees

TBV separates routine governance, emergency controls, and Bitcoin payout protection. A committee name does not grant a permission. The deployed roles, timelocks, and vault parameters define each permission.

> **Mainnet review status:** Membership, signature thresholds, deployment addresses, and timelock delays still need launch confirmation. This page describes the planned role model, not a deployed configuration.

## Who does what

| Group | Layer | Responsibility | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Interim Governance | Ethereum | Proposes contract and parameter changes through the timelock executors. Uses assigned pause and freeze roles. | Cannot skip a timelock for actions that require it. |
| Emergency Guardian | Ethereum | Cancels queued proposals and uses assigned pause, unpause, freeze, and unfreeze controls. | Its emergency role does not grant unrestricted upgrade authority. |
| Freeze Stewards | Ethereum | Use limited freeze controls for incident response. | Do not assume an unfreeze permission. Confirm the deployed role before release. |
| Bitcoin Security Council | Bitcoin | Signs council no-payout transactions to block a malicious claim. | Cannot choose another BTC destination or restore a lost key. |
| ETH Light Client Upgrade Committee | Bitcoin | Authorizes verification-key changes through Bitcoin governance transactions and the notice mechanism. | This is separate from an Ethereum contract upgrade. |
| Aave governance | Aave | Controls the Aave-side permissions and risk controls that apply to the integrated market. | These controls are separate from TBV governance. |

The deployer assigns the initial roles during deployment. The launch review must confirm that the deployer no longer holds temporary privileges.

Vault Providers, App Keepers, and Universal Challengers are protocol operators. They are not governance committees merely because they operate services. See [Protocol actors](./protocol-actors.mdx).

## Routine changes

1. Identify the target contract and the proposed change.
2. Check the proposer and executor roles for that target.
3. Read the queued proposal and its execution time.
4. Check whether an authorized guardian cancels the proposal.
5. Read the execution receipt before treating the change as active.

A scheduled proposal is not an executed change. Signature thresholds and delays can differ between committees and action types.

## Emergency controls

A **freeze** restricts selected Ethereum operations. A **pause** can restrict a wider set. The exact effect depends on the contract and function.

Do not assume that repayment, withdrawal, or liquidation remains available during every emergency state. Check the affected contract and incident notice.

Ethereum pause permissions do not come from the Bitcoin parameter `councilQuorum`. That parameter defines a Bitcoin council signature threshold.

A Bitcoin council action blocks a payout. It does not move the BTC to a new address. A blocked payout can delay access to funds.

An Ethereum pause does not change Bitcoin spending conditions already committed to a vault. Each recovery path still needs its own keys, artifacts, timing, and protocol conditions.

## Verification-key changes

The ETH Light Client Upgrade Committee and Ethereum governance have different responsibilities. A Bitcoin verification-key authorization does not itself update every Ethereum parameter.

Check the Bitcoin authorization, notice period, and related Ethereum parameter version before using a new circuit version. Existing vaults retain their recorded protocol snapshot. Do not assume that a proposal automatically migrates them.

## What users should check

- The network, contract addresses, and current role holders.
- The proposal status and applicable delay.
- The vault's recorded parameter and participant versions.
- The effect of any freeze, pause, or council action on the requested operation.
- The recovery material needed for that vault.

Use [Mainnet addresses](../network-info/mainnet/contract-addresses.mdx) and [Mainnet parameters](../network-info/mainnet/protocol-parameters-and-caps.mdx) for confirmed deployment values.

## Launch confirmation checklist

Before release, confirm the member list, threshold, address, and permissions for each group. Confirm each timelock delay and the process for replacing a role holder.

Resolve the Freeze Steward scope before describing unfreeze access. Check the available user actions during each emergency state against the deployed contracts.

Do not publish candidate member lists or proposed thresholds as confirmed values.

See [Safety and trust assumptions](../start-here/safety-and-trust-assumptions.mdx) and [Known limitations](../security/known-limitations.mdx).
