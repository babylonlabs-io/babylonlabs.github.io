Security is the core promise of TBV: the design is *trustless*, and this page is where that claim is meant to be independently checked. Because mainnet holds real BTC, TBV is reviewed as a whole system — the on-chain contracts **and** the cryptographic protocol that lets Bitcoin enforce the vault.

## Scope of review

Audits cover the full trust surface, not just the Solidity:

- **On-chain contracts.** The core protocol (`BTCVaultRegistry`, `ProtocolParams`, `ApplicationRegistry`, `CapPolicy`, `FeeEscrow`, `AllowListPolicy`) and the Aave v4 application (`AaveAdapter`, `AaveAdapterPositionProxy`, `BTCVaultSwap`, `VaultBTC`), including the UUPS upgrade paths and role/guardian gating.
- **Pre-signed Bitcoin transaction graph.** The Pre-PegIn HTLC and the graph of pre-signed Bitcoin transactions that back peg-in, activation, redemption, and self-recovery — the mechanism that makes the vault enforceable on Bitcoin itself.
- **WOTS commitments.** The Winternitz one-time signature commitments the depositor and operators bind to, and their role in self-claim and challenge paths.
- **BABE construction.** The witness-encryption / garbled-Groth16 construction (with WOTS integration and the cut-and-choose setup) that lets Bitcoin verify proofs cheaply.
- **SP1 + Groth16 proof pipeline.** The zk proving pipeline (SP1 zkVM proofs wrapped to Groth16) consumed on-chain and by the challenge/verification flow.
