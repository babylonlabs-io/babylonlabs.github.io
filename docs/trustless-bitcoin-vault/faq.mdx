# Trustless Bitcoin Vaults FAQ

## Babylon general

### Has any of this been audited?

Yes. TBV has undergone independent security reviews across the full system,
including its Solidity smart contracts, Aave v4 integration, BTCVault,
zero-knowledge provers and circuits, web services, and BABE cryptographic
components.

Independent security engagements have involved Coinspect, Sherlock, Zellic,
ABDK, ZKSecurity, and Runtime Verification. Findings from these reviews are
handled through Babylon Labs' security and release processes.

Security is a top priority at Babylon and a continuous commitment throughout
TBV's development. We plan to commission additional independent audits as the
protocol evolves and expand security coverage across its components. Babylon
already maintains a
[public bug bounty program](https://immunefi.com/bug-bounty/babylon-labs/information/),
and we plan to include TBV within its scope.

We welcome responsible disclosure from the security community. If you believe
you have identified a security issue affecting TBV, please report it privately
to [security@babylonlabs.io](mailto:security@babylonlabs.io) so our security
team can investigate it promptly.

### What is Babylon's track record?

Babylon launched the Bitcoin Staking Protocol on mainnet in April 2025. It has
been the largest Bitcoin-based project by TVL, with assets staked above \$7B at
peak. TBV is the next protocol built on that same trustless infrastructure, to
enable native Bitcoin as collateral.

## Overview

### What are Trustless Bitcoin Vaults (TBV)?

Babylon Trustless Bitcoin Vaults (TBV) let native Bitcoin be used as
collateral on any chain and in any application, without wrapping, bridging, or
intermediaries. TBV-integrated applications can use native Bitcoin collateral
for lending, stablecoins, credit cards, derivatives, insurance, and other
financial products.

### How does TBV work?

A depositor locks native Bitcoin into a BTCVault in a UTXO on Bitcoin. After
the vault is verified, the protocol creates a 1:1 collateral record
representing that BTCVault on an integrated chain. Applications such as Aave
v4 can recognize this collateral and allow borrowing against the underlying
native Bitcoin.

### What are the main benefits of using TBV to use my native Bitcoin as collateral?

There are a few notable benefits of TBV:

* **Native collateral.** You can borrow against native Bitcoin on the Bitcoin
  network. TBV does not require wrapping, bridging, or intermediaries, which
  allows you to retain title to your Bitcoin.
* **Cryptographic verifiability.** The protocol relies on Bitcoin Script,
  cryptographic proofs, and protocol rules rather than custodial
  intermediaries to enforce collateral conditions.
* **Capital efficiency.** TBV supports any applications, such as borrowing and
  lending, stablecoins, and various financial products. This programmability
  improves the capital efficiency of native Bitcoin collateral.

### Why Babylon + Aave?

Babylon provides the native Bitcoin collateral infrastructure. Aave provides
one of the largest lending protocols in DeFi, with over \$10B liquidity in
TVL. Together, the partnership allows Bitcoin holders to trustlessly borrow
against their native Bitcoin.

### Why are launch caps so small?

Mainnet is designed to roll out in phases. Initial BTCVault and borrowing caps
are intentionally conservative to ensure the best and most secure experience,
and may be adjusted over time as additional phases are introduced.

### What is the roadmap beyond Aave?

Native Bitcoin-backed borrowing on Aave v4 is the first use case of TBV. In
the near future, TBV-integrated applications can use native Bitcoin collateral
for lending, BTC-backed stablecoins, credit cards, derivatives, insurance,
options DEX, and other financial products.

## Security, custody, and trust model

### Who holds my Bitcoin?

Your own self-custodial BTCVault holds the Bitcoin. A BTCVault is a UTXO
script on the Bitcoin chain that locks your Bitcoin. It makes sure that:

* You can unilaterally unlock and withdraw your Bitcoin anytime as long as
  doing so will not cause under-collateralization of your loan.
* No one can move your Bitcoin, unless you are liquidated by Aave.

### Is my collateral segregated or pooled?

Your collateral is segregated. Each BTCVault belongs to a single Depositor and
holds only that Depositor's Bitcoin. Funds are not pooled or commingled with
those of other users.

### Can my Bitcoin be rehypothecated?

No. Your Bitcoin remains locked inside your own BTCVault under predefined
spending conditions. The protocol does not and cannot transfer custody of your
Bitcoin to a lending pool or intermediary.

### Is wrapped or bridged Bitcoin required?

No. TBV uses native Bitcoin as collateral. It does not require wrapped Bitcoin
or bridges.

### How is my native Bitcoin on Bitcoin used as collateral on Ethereum?

You lock native Bitcoin into a BTCVault on the Bitcoin chain. After the
BTCVault is verified and activated, applications such as Aave v4 recognize
that as collateral and can allow borrowing against the underlying Bitcoin
while it remains locked on Bitcoin.

### What enforces the rules?

The protocol's spending conditions are enforced through Bitcoin Script,
zero-knowledge proofs ([BABE](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/065.pdf)), and a
challenge mechanism that allows invalid redemption or liquidation claims to be
disputed before Bitcoin is released. These mechanisms replace reliance on a
trusted intermediary with cryptographic verification.

### What happens if Babylon or my Vault Provider goes offline?

TBV is designed so that redemption and verification do not depend on Babylon
remaining online. Independent protocol participants, including Universal
Challengers, can dispute invalid claims. Depositors may also operate their own
Vault Provider. There is also a Security Council that is available only as an
emergency backstop for exceptional situations.

### Is there counterparty credit risk?

TBV is designed so you do not rely on an intermediary to hold your Bitcoin.
However, users remain exposed to risks associated with smart contracts,
protocol implementation, price oracles, and the integrated lending application
they choose to use.

### What can Babylon do to my funds? What if Babylon disappears?

Babylon cannot unilaterally move, seize, or redeem your Bitcoin. Redemption
and liquidation follow protocol-defined rules enforced through Bitcoin Script,
cryptographic proofs, and the challenge process, not by any company.

### Do I have to trust my Vault Provider?

No. A Vault Provider helps administer BTCVault creation and redemption, but it
cannot spend your Bitcoin outside the protocol's predefined conditions.
Universal Challengers can dispute invalid claims, and users may also choose to
operate their own Vault Provider if they prefer.

### What enforces correctness across two chains?

Cross-chain correctness is enforced through Bitcoin Script, BABE
zero-knowledge proofs, and a challenge period. Before Bitcoin is released from
a BTCVault, the required proof is verified and participants have an
opportunity to challenge any invalid claim.
[Read more in the BABE paper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/065.pdf).

### Who are the operators and how are they separated?

TBV separates responsibilities across different operator roles, each limited
to specific functions:

* **Vault Provider:** An infrastructure operator appointed by the depositor to
  administer the vault's creation and process redemption.
* **Universal Challenger:** A global set of operators that monitor claims
  across every BTCVault, in every application, and challenge any that are
  invalid.
* **App Keeper:** Every integrated application appoints its own set of App
  Keepers. App Keepers hold both claim and challenge rights for that
  application's BTCVaults, such as the Arbitrageur in the Aave v4 integration.

## Deposit and vaults

### How does a vault work?

When you deposit native Bitcoin, it is locked into your own BTCVault. A
pre-signed transaction graph defines the valid ways the BTCVault can be spent
under the protocol rules. Once the vault is verified, TBV-integrated
applications such as Aave v4 recognize the underlying Bitcoin as collateral.

### What is fixed when a vault is created?

When a BTCVault is created, several properties are fixed for the lifetime of
that vault, including:

* Who is authorized to redeem the Bitcoin
* The BTCVault being redeemed as a whole
* Which integrated application governs redemption rights
* Who is authorized to challenge invalid redemption claims

These properties cannot be changed after vault creation.

### How long does vault creation take? Why does it take so long?

On testnet, a BTCVault becomes active about two hours after the deposit. That
time is almost entirely spent on Bitcoin confirmations, as the protocol has to
wait before the BTCVault can be activated (12 confirmations at roughly 10
minutes each). Once those confirmations are complete, the remaining protocol
steps generally finish within several minutes.

This may not be something everyone is familiar with, as transaction
confirmation on Bitcoin usually takes longer than on other networks. If you
encounter any errors, please join
[our Discord](https://discord.com/invite/babylonglobal) to request a support
ticket.

### Why split Bitcoin into two vaults?

Each BTCVault is backed by a single Bitcoin UTXO and cannot be partially
seized during liquidation. Splitting your collateral across multiple BTCVaults
allows the protocol to liquidate only the vaults needed to restore the
required health factor, instead of liquidating your entire position.

The application recommends an appropriate BTCVault split based on the current
protocol parameters. The app dashboard will suggest this split automatically.
See
[Liquidation risk](/trustless-bitcoin-vault/use-for-lending/liquidation-risk/#why-a-btc-vault-is-indivisible)
for the detailed mechanism.

### How do I change the vault order?

While your BTCVaults are "In Use", you can reorder them from the Collateral
section of the application. During liquidation, BTCVaults are considered in
the order you specify, beginning with the first vault in the list. Changing
the order requires an Ethereum transaction. BTCVaults that are "Available" and
not securing a borrowing position are not included in the liquidation order.

### What if vault creation stalls or expires?

If BTCVault creation does not complete successfully, the protocol provides
recovery paths depending on where the process stopped.

* If the off-chain setup does not complete within approximately 24 hours, the
  BTCVault expires and the BTCVault activation fee is refunded automatically.
* If activation does not occur within approximately 48 hours after BTCVault
  creation, the fee is non-refundable, but your Bitcoin remains recoverable.

In both cases, you can recover your Bitcoin after the protocol timelock by
signing with the original Bitcoin key.

### Why am I asked to download claimer artifacts?

The WOTS (Winternitz One-Time Signature) keypair and associated claimer
artifacts enable the self-claim recovery path. If your Vault Provider becomes
unavailable during redemption, these files allow you to complete the
redemption process yourself using the protocol's self-claim procedure.

Store these files securely alongside your wallet recovery phrase. If they are
lost, recovery may require assistance through the protocol's emergency
procedures and may require Security Council escalation. See
[Withdraw & redeem](/trustless-bitcoin-vault/use-for-lending/withdraw-and-redeem/#how-to-self-claim)
for the self-claim path.

### Does every spend get pre-signed before my Bitcoin moves?

Yes. During BTCVault creation, the protocol establishes the complete
pre-signed Bitcoin transaction graph before your Bitcoin is deposited into the
vault. These pre-signed transactions define every valid spending path
permitted by the protocol.

## Borrowing and redemption

### What can I borrow, and where?

Through the current TBV integration with Aave v4, you can use your native
Bitcoin collateral to borrow ERC-20 assets supported by that Aave market.

### How do I get my Bitcoin back? How long does it take?

You can redeem your Bitcoin after repaying enough debt to satisfy the
protocol's collateral requirements. Once redemption begins, the protocol
enters a challenge period of approximately three days. If no valid challenge
is raised during that period, your Bitcoin is released to the Bitcoin address
associated with your BTCVault.

### How does redemption work?

When you initiate redemption, the protocol generates the required proof that
the redemption conditions have been satisfied. A claim is then submitted on
Bitcoin, followed by a challenge period during which invalid claims can be
disputed. If the claim is not successfully challenged, the Bitcoin is released
according to the BTCVault's predefined spending conditions.

### How is the interest rate set?

Borrowing interest rates are determined by Aave v4, and you can check the
current rate on [Aavescan](https://aavescan.com/).

### Why does repayment ask for two wallet confirmations?

Repaying an ERC-20 debt involves two transactions:

* First, you approve the lending protocol to spend the repayment token.
* Second, you submit the transaction that repays the debt.

### Can someone else repay my debt?

Yes. Another address can repay debt on your behalf, subject to the lending
protocol's rules.

### Why is my borrowing rate different?

There may be different situations where:

* The displayed rate is annual (APR), while interest accrues continuously.
* Small differences may also result from accrued interest between transactions
  or normal rounding when repaying outstanding debt.

### Why does withdrawal take about 3 days?

Bitcoin cannot natively and directly verify Ethereum state. TBV uses BABE
zero-knowledge proofs together with a challenge period of approximately three
days before Bitcoin can be released. This allows invalid redemption claims to
be disputed before settlement is finalized.

### Why is Withdraw disabled for one of my vaults?

A BTCVault cannot be withdrawn if removing it would cause your remaining
borrowing position to fall below the required collateral threshold. The
depositor must repay sufficient debt first, and the vault will then become
eligible for withdrawal.

### Why does the dashboard still show Pending Withdraw?

The Ethereum transaction only initiates redemption. After it is confirmed, the
protocol runs the Bitcoin side of the claim, and the challenge window starts
before Bitcoin is released. During this time, the dashboard continues to
display "Pending Withdraw" for the full duration, which is roughly 3 days from
the withdrawal request to the actual Bitcoin arrival in your wallet on
Bitcoin.

### The Vault Provider is unresponsive during redemption. What now?

If your Vault Provider becomes unavailable during redemption, you can use the
protocol's self-claim process, provided you retained your WOTS keypair and
claimer artifacts created during BTCVault setup. This allows you to complete
redemption independently without relying on the Vault Provider. See
[Withdraw & redeem](/trustless-bitcoin-vault/use-for-lending/withdraw-and-redeem/)
for the full self-claim walkthrough.

### Where does the borrowing happen and who sets the terms?

Borrowing takes place through the current Aave v4 integration, and you can
check the current terms on [Aavescan](https://aavescan.com/).

### Why is there a 3-day challenge window, and can it be shortened?

TBV uses a ZK proof (via BABE) plus a ~72-hour challenge period to verify
native Bitcoin on Ethereum, so any Vault Keeper can dispute an invalid claim
before Bitcoin is released. It is a fixed security parameter tied to the
fraud-proof design, which cannot be shortened per user.

### What happens to my position if Aave DAO freezes or pauses the Spoke?

If Aave DAO pauses the Spoke, actions such as borrowing, repaying,
withdrawing, and liquidation are blocked. If it freezes the Spoke, existing
positions can still repay, withdraw, or be liquidated, but new borrowing or
collateral cannot be added.

## Liquidation

### What is the health factor and what number is safe?

The health factor measures how close your borrowing position is to
liquidation. It compares the risk-adjusted value of your collateral with your
outstanding debt:

```text
Health Factor = (Collateral Value * Collateral Factor) / Debt Value
```

A position becomes eligible for liquidation when its health factor drops below
1.0.

| Health factor | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Above 1.5 | Comfortable margin |
| 1.2 to 1.5 | Approaching risk |
| 1.0 to 1.2 | High risk |
| Below 1.0 | Liquidatable |

The ranges shown in the application (for example, above 1.5 or between 1.2
and 1.5) are informational guidance only. The protocol enforces only the
liquidation threshold.

### What happens during liquidation?

If your position falls below the liquidation threshold defined by the lending
market, a liquidator repays part or all of your debt and the protocol seizes
enough Bitcoin collateral to cover it. In return, the protocol transfers
enough BTCVaults to satisfy the liquidation according to the configured vault
order. Because each BTCVault corresponds to a single Bitcoin UTXO, liquidation
always transfers the whole BTCVault rather than fractions of a vault.

If multiple BTCVaults secure your position, only the vaults required for the
liquidation are transferred. Any remaining BTCVaults continue securing your
position if debt remains outstanding.

### What is the fairness payment?

Because a BTCVault is indivisible, a liquidation may seize slightly more
Bitcoin than needed. The fairness mechanism compensates you for the
difference: it either reduces your remaining debt or, if there is surplus left
after your debt is covered, pays it to you in WBTC.

### Who can liquidate my position, and is it discretionary?

Liquidation is rule-based, not discretionary. If your position reaches the
liquidation threshold defined by the lending market, permissionless
liquidators repay the debt and receive the vault collateral in return. Neither
Babylon nor any other party decides when a position is liquidated.

### How does partial liquidation work on an asset that has no denominations?

Each BTCVault is a single, indivisible Bitcoin UTXO, so liquidation always
seizes whole BTCVaults, never a fraction. To support more granular
liquidation, users may split their collateral across multiple BTCVaults.
During liquidation, the protocol transfers only the BTCVaults required to
restore the target health factor, following the user-defined vault order. Any
over-seizure resulting from indivisible BTCVaults is handled through the
fairness payment mechanism.

### What happens if I get liquidated?

If your position is liquidated, a liquidator repays part or all of your debt
and receives the required BTCVault collateral according to the protocol rules.
Any surplus value comes back to you through the fairness payment.

## Wallets and setup

### Which wallets do I need?

You need two wallets: a Bitcoin wallet and an Ethereum wallet. To use the
testnet app, connect them to Bitcoin Signet and Ethereum Sepolia respectively.

* The Bitcoin wallet must produce Taproot (P2TR) addresses and support PSBT
  signing plus message signing (BIP-322 or ECDSA).
* The Ethereum wallet must support the Ethereum network.

Any wallet that meets these requirements works; UniSat is one supported
example. For the current supported wallets, networks, and faucet links,
[go to the testnet app](https://btc-vaults.testnet.babylonlabs.io/) and click
"Connect Wallet".

### Why do I need a Taproot address?

TBV locks Bitcoin into Taproot (P2TR) outputs. To create a BTCVault, your
Bitcoin wallet must be able to generate Taproot addresses. Bitcoin address
types maintain separate UTXOs, so funds held on other address types are not
automatically available through a Taproot address.

### I cannot connect my wallets. What should I do?

Check the following:

* Your Bitcoin wallet is set to Signet, not mainnet.
* Your Ethereum wallet is on Sepolia, not another network.
* Both wallets are unlocked before opening the testnet app.
* If several Bitcoin extensions are installed, disable the ones not in use so
  the app detects the correct one.
* Refresh the page once and reconnect the desired wallets.

If you are using a multisig wallet (for example, Safe through WalletConnect),
the signing flow may not be supported. A browser extension wallet may be
required.

### Why is my balance not showing?

You may be experiencing one of the following issues:

* The connected wallet address differs from the one holding the funds. Try to
  reconnect with the correct address.
* The Signet faucet transaction is not yet confirmed. Bitcoin confirmations
  are required before the balance becomes spendable. This typically takes a
  few minutes on Signet.

### What if I lose my WOTS keypair file and claimer artifacts?

If your Vault Provider remains available, losing your WOTS keypair and claimer
artifacts does not immediately affect normal redemption. However, you would no
longer be able to use the self-claim recovery path. Recovery in this situation
may require the Security Council. Visit the
[Security Council](/trustless-bitcoin-vault/technical-details/protocol-actors/#security-council)
section to learn more.

### What if I refresh the page while signing?

The deposit flow is designed to resume after a page refresh. Reopen the
dashboard with the same Bitcoin and Ethereum wallets used for the vault.
Refresh once and look for any pending deposit or existing vault on the
dashboard. Do not switch to a different Bitcoin address for a deposit already
in progress.

If the flow does not resume,
[contact support on Discord](https://discord.com/invite/babylonglobal) with
screenshots, wallet addresses, and any transaction hashes.

### What if I lose my Bitcoin key?

Your BTCVault is secured by your Bitcoin private key. If you permanently lose
that key, you lose access to the Bitcoin in the same way you would lose access
to any self-custodial Bitcoin wallet.

Back up your wallet seed phrase, along with your WOTS keypair file and claimer
artifacts, at vault creation so you can recover independently.

## Getting started

### Where can I get more information about native Bitcoin-backed borrowing with Aave v4 and public testnet?

For technical documentation, see
[What is TBV](/trustless-bitcoin-vault/start-here/what-is-tbv/).

To try native Bitcoin-backed borrowing powered by TBV with Aave v4 on testnet,
visit our app:
[https://btc-vaults.testnet.babylonlabs.io/](https://btc-vaults.testnet.babylonlabs.io/)

We welcome your feedback. Mainnet is targeted for Q4 2026.

### What signing capability do I need?

Depositors need a Bitcoin wallet that supports Taproot (P2TR) addresses, PSBT
signing, and message signing (BIP-322 or ECDSA), together with an Ethereum
wallet.

Operators such as Vault Providers and App Keepers require Bitcoin and Ethereum
signing infrastructure capable of participating in the protocol's pre-signing
process.

### How many user interactions does a deposit take?

Users need to actively interact with their wallets during three sessions.
Usually it takes between 6 and 10 device clicks:

* **Start the deposit:** Sign the transactions to register the deposit, and
  link your Bitcoin and Ethereum wallets.
* **After Bitcoin confirmations:** Return to sign the payout and recovery
  transactions once the required Bitcoin confirmations have been reached.
* **Activate the vault:** Retrieve the deposit secret, sign the activation
  transaction, and complete the vault activation. After the final Bitcoin
  confirmation, the vault is ready to use as collateral.

Between these signing sessions, the protocol waits for Bitcoin confirmations
and Vault Provider verification. Users can safely close the application during
these waiting periods and return later to continue the deposit.

### Can my users use a hardware wallet?

Yes, provided the hardware wallet supports Taproot (P2TR) addresses together
with PSBT signing and the required message signing capabilities used by the
protocol.
