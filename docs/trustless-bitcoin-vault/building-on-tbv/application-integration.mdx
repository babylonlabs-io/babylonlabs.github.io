## TBV is built for many applications

TBV is not a single lending product. It is a Bitcoin-collateral primitive that many DeFi applications can consume. The vault layer locks BTC on Bitcoin behind pre-signed paths and makes it usable on Ethereum without a bridge, a wrapped token, or a custodian — and it is deliberately application-agnostic.

**Aave v4 borrowing is the first application.** It is the reference implementation of how a product plugs into the vault layer. **Morpho Blue is the next planned application.** Stablecoins, perpetual DEXs, options, cross-chain markets, and staking dual-utility are on the roadmap. All of these can integrate without changing the vault layer.

An **application** is the deepest way to build on TBV. Unlike a Resolver (which rides on the existing Aave app), an application registers with the protocol directly and owns its own collateral semantics: how the locked BTC is priced, what borrowing or minting it backs, how liquidation works, and what its own accounting token represents.

## How a new application plugs in

A new application does two things:

1. **Registers with `ApplicationRegistry`.** The protocol's `ApplicationRegistry` gates which applications are allowed to consume vaults. Registration is governance-approved and is expected to follow review and audit of the application's contracts.

2. **Implements `IApplicationEntryPoint`.** Every application entry point implements a single activation hook that `BTCVaultRegistry` calls when a depositor activates a vault into that application:

   ```solidity
   function activateVault(
       bytes32 vaultId,
       BTCVaultTypes.BTCVaultBasicInfo memory vault,
       bytes memory activationMetadata
   ) external;
   ```

   When a depositor activates a vault, `BTCVaultRegistry` forwards the vault ID, the basic vault info, and any application-specific `activationMetadata` to your entry point. From there, your application takes over: it records the collateral, mints or credits whatever internal accounting your product uses, and wires the vault into your risk model. Applications that need protocol-specific vault fields beyond `BTCVaultBasicInfo` query `BTCVaultRegistry` directly.

The `AaveAdapter` is the worked example of an `IApplicationEntryPoint` implementation: on activation it assigns the vault to a position account, supplies it as Aave collateral, and mints its internal `vaultBTC` accounting unit. Your application implements the equivalent for your product.

## What the full guide will cover

The held guide walks through, in detail:

- **When to build an application vs a Resolver vs just using the SDK** — choosing the right integration depth by product type.
- **The vault-consumer interface** — the activation hook, redemption and liquidation triggers, and the vault ownership and seizure model.
- **Writing an application adapter** — your equivalent of `AaveAdapter`, including per-depositor position accounting and redemption routing.
- **Designing your accounting token** — why `vaultBTC` is Aave-internal (not a shared primitive), and the invariants a new app's accounting token must preserve.
- **`ApplicationRegistry` & governance approval** — the review, audit, and registration path, and how caps are assigned per application.
- **Security responsibilities** — the exact line between what the protocol guarantees (non-custody, pre-signed paths, fraud monitoring) and what the integrating app must own (oracle, health factor, liquidation economics, upgrade and pause).
