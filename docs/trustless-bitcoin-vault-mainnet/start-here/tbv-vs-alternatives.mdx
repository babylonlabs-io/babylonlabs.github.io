---
title: TBV vs alternatives
---

Every way of using Bitcoin in DeFi answers one question: **what do you have to trust for your BTC to stay yours?** Wrapped tokens and bridges answer it with an institution or a committee. TBV answers it with Bitcoin consensus and a proof system. This page is written for institutional readers assessing the "trustless" claim, so it is deliberate about where trust actually sits in each design.

## The short version

- **WBTC / cbBTC** — a custodian holds your BTC and issues an ERC-20 IOU. Convenient and liquid, but the backing is only as good as the custodian, and the token can de-peg if the custodian fails.
- **BitVM bridges** — remove the single custodian but introduce a signer/operator committee and a bridge contract that holds pooled funds; you trust that at least one honest operator and the committee's liveness/economics.
- **Just HODL** — perfectly trustless, but your BTC is idle: no yield, no borrowing, no composability.
- **TBV** — your BTC stays in your own Bitcoin Taproot script, released only when a zero-knowledge proof of the correct Ethereum state is verified on Bitcoin. No custodian, no wrapped derivative, no pooled bridge — and it is still usable as collateral.

## Comparison

| | WBTC | cbBTC | BitVM bridges | Just HODL | **Trustless Bitcoin Vault** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Where the BTC lives** | Custodian's wallet | Custodian's wallet | Bridge / operator-controlled | Your wallet | **Your Bitcoin Taproot script** |
| **Custody** | Third-party custodian | Third-party custodian | Operator/committee (pooled) | Self | **Self-custodial** |
| **What you trust** | The custodian's solvency & honesty | The custodian's solvency & honesty | Committee + ≥1 honest operator + liveness | Bitcoin only | **Bitcoin consensus + ZK proof system** |
| **Wrapped/derivative token?** | Yes (ERC-20 IOU) | Yes (ERC-20 IOU) | Yes (bridged representation) | No | **No** — `vaultBTC` is a non-transferable internal accounting unit, not a tradable wrapper |
| **De-peg / backing-break risk** | Custodian failure breaks peg | Custodian failure breaks peg | Bridge exploit / operator failure | None | **No pooled backing to break; release is proof-gated** |
| **BTC release condition** | Custodian honors redemption | Custodian honors redemption | Committee/operators sign | You spend it | **ZK proof verified on Bitcoin (BABE)** |
| **Usable as DeFi collateral** | Yes | Yes | Yes | No | **Yes (Aave v4 today)** |
| **Depositor recovery if operator disappears** | Rely on custodian | Rely on custodian | Rely on committee | N/A | **Self-refund at peg-in; self-claim at peg-out** |

## Why the "trustless" claim holds

- **Self-custodial by construction.** Your Bitcoin key is required at vault creation and on every release path. Every legitimate spend is pre-signed into the vault's transaction graph; afterward no party — including the Vault Provider — can invent a new way to move your BTC. There is no account a custodian can freeze and no honest-majority you must assume for custody.
- **No bridge, no wrap.** The BTC is never sent to another chain and never represented by a tradable derivative whose backing could diverge from reserves. `vaultBTC` is minted on Ethereum only as an 8-decimal, non-transferable internal accounting unit so an application can measure your collateral; it cannot be sold, moved, or de-pegged, and it is burned when you redeem.
- **Cryptographic verification, on Bitcoin.** Withdrawal unlocks only when a zero-knowledge proof of the correct Ethereum redemption state is verified **on Bitcoin itself**, using the BABE construction (BitVM3-style garbled circuits with Winternitz one-time-signature commitments). A dishonest claim can be challenged and slashed during the challenge window; an honest one settles. No soft fork and no trusted relay is involved.
- **Recovery does not depend on an operator.** If peg-in activation stalls, you reclaim your funds via the Pre-PegIn refund path. If the Vault Provider is unresponsive at peg-out, you complete the payout yourself with your WOTS keypair and claimer artifacts. Trust in operators is therefore **optional and removable**, not structural.

## How to read this if you hold BTC

- **You want maximum liquidity and are comfortable with custodial risk** → WBTC / cbBTC are the simplest, at the cost of trusting a custodian.
- **You want zero counterparty risk and don't need yield** → just HODL.
- **You want to keep Bitcoin-level custody guarantees *and* use your BTC as collateral** → TBV is the design that removes the custodian and the wrapper instead of relabeling them.

Note that TBV is on **Beta Mainnet**: real value, but capped, allowlisted, and still hardening. The trust properties above are structural; the operational maturity is still growing. See **Beta Mainnet: what to expect** and, for the full risk surface, **Safety & trust assumptions**.
