title: How it works

TBV turns native Bitcoin into DeFi collateral in three conceptual stages: **peg-in** locks your BTC on the Bitcoin Network and activates a vault, **use as collateral** mints `vaultBTC` so an integrated application (Aave v4 today) can borrow against it, and **peg-out** releases your BTC on Bitcoin once a redemption is proven and the challenge window clears. Throughout, the real BTC never leaves Bitcoin and no custodian ever holds it.

On Beta Mainnet this is a **real-value** flow: peg-ins lock real BTC and pay real Bitcoin fees, and borrowing and liquidation are economically live. Where an exact figure depends on launch configuration it is marked **to be announced at launch**; see **Beta Mainnet: what to expect** and **Network Info**.

## Stage 1 — Peg-in: lock BTC and activate the vault

Peg-in moves your BTC into a Taproot vault **without handing it to anyone**. You co-sign the vault's pre-signed transaction graph at creation, so every legitimate spend — redemption, liquidation redemption, refund — is fixed up front and no new spend can be invented afterward.

1. **Fund the Pre-PegIn output.** You broadcast a Bitcoin transaction to a Pre-PegIn Taproot address whose script paths are agreed with the Vault Provider. This output carries a **refund path** back to you (a hash timelock, `tRefund`) so your funds are never stranded if activation does not complete.
2. **Wait for confirmations.** The protocol requires the Pre-PegIn output to reach a minimum confirmation depth on Bitcoin before it is eligible for activation (the exact depth is **to be announced at launch**).
3. **Activate via an HTLC atomic swap.** Activation is a hashlock/secret exchange. Your vault is registered on Ethereum with a **hashlock**; when you call `activateVaultWithSecret` on `BTCVaultRegistry` and reveal the matching **secret**, the reveal simultaneously (a) transitions the vault to Active on Ethereum and (b) gives the Vault Provider the preimage it needs to complete the peg-in on Bitcoin. Neither side can take the other's asset without the swap completing — that is the "atomic" property.
4. **Vault is Active.** `BTCVaultRegistry` now holds the vault's lifecycle state, your hashlock, and your WOTS commitment, and calls `activateVault(...)` on the registered application so it can begin collateral accounting.

There is a bounded window at each step. If activation stalls, you keep the ability to reclaim the Pre-PegIn output via its refund path — you do not depend on any operator to get your BTC back. See **Safety & trust assumptions** for the exact windows and recovery paths.

## Stage 2 — Use `vaultBTC` as collateral on Aave v4

When a vault activates, the Aave v4 application (`AaveAdapter`) mints **`vaultBTC`** to represent the locked BTC. `vaultBTC` is an 8-decimal, **non-transferable, non-wrapped internal accounting unit** — it exists only inside the integration to track your collateral. It is not a wrapped BTC and cannot be moved or sold on the open market; the real BTC stays on Bitcoin.

- **Isolated position.** Each depositor borrows through a dedicated `AaveAdapterPositionProxy`, so your position is isolated from other depositors'.
- **Borrow.** With `vaultBTC` supplied as collateral on the Babylon spoke of Aave v4, you can borrow available assets against it (for example a stablecoin or WBTC) using `borrowFromCorePosition(...)`, and repay with `repayToCorePosition(...)`. Your borrowing capacity is governed by Aave's risk parameters plus TBV's on-chain caps.
- **Multiple vaults, one position.** You can hold several vaults behind one position and manage their ordering with `reorderVaults(...)`; you withdraw collateral vaults with `withdrawCollaterals(...)`.
- **Liquidation is partial-position.** If your health factor falls too low, the protocol does **not** seize everything. It seizes only the **minimum whole-vault prefix** needed to restore the Aave spoke's `targetHealthFactor`, and any over-seizure surplus is returned to you through the fairness mechanism. Seized vaults are redeemed through the same proven peg-out path described below.

Caps apply from day one: `CapPolicy` enforces a per-application total (`totalCapBTC`) and a per-address cap (`perAddressCapBTC`), and `ProtocolParams` enforces a per-vault maximum (`maxPegInAmount`). Launch values are **to be announced at launch**.

## Stage 3 — Peg-out: prove redemption and release BTC

Peg-out returns your BTC to Bitcoin once you are entitled to it — after you repay and redeem, or after a liquidation redeems on your behalf. The release is **cryptographically gated**: the Bitcoin script will only unlock when a zero-knowledge proof of the correct Ethereum redemption state is verified **on Bitcoin itself**, through the BABE construction (BitVM3-style garbled circuits with Winternitz one-time-signature commitments). No soft fork and no trusted relay is required.

The peg-out follows a claim–challenge game:

1. **Redeem on Ethereum.** You repay outstanding debt and redeem the vault; the application burns the corresponding `vaultBTC` and `BTCVaultRegistry` records that this vault is entitled to a payout on Bitcoin.
2. **Claim.** The Vault Provider (or you, in the fallback below) posts the redemption **claim** and **asserts** the ZK proof of the Ethereum state on Bitcoin.
3. **Challenge window.** A time-boxed window opens (governed by `timelockAssert` / `timelockChallengeAssert`) during which any watcher — notably the Universal Challenger — can challenge a false assertion. An honest, correct assertion survives; a fraudulent one is refuted and slashed.
4. **Payout.** When the challenge window closes with no successful challenge, the payout transaction is broadcast and your BTC settles to your Bitcoin address.
5. **Self-claim fallback.** If the Vault Provider is unresponsive, you are not stuck: with your WOTS keypair and claimer artifacts you can drive the claim yourself and complete the payout without the operator. This is why backing up those artifacts is essential — see **Safety & trust assumptions**.

Challenge-window durations and confirmation depths are configured for mainnet and are **to be announced at launch**.

## The actors at a glance

| Actor | What they do | Can the depositor do it instead? |
|---|---|---|
| **Depositor (you)** | Create and co-sign the vault, activate via secret, borrow/repay, redeem, and reclaim BTC. Holds the WOTS keypair and claimer artifacts. | — |
| **Vault Provider (VP)** | Co-signs the vault graph, completes peg-in on Bitcoin, and normally posts the peg-out claim. Invite-only during Beta. | Yes — self-refund at peg-in and self-claim at peg-out. |
| **Application Vault Keeper (AVK)** | Per-application keeper; acts as the arbitrageur inside Aave (handles seized-vault redemption and rebalancing). | No — but liquidation fairness protects your surplus. |
| **Universal Challenger (UC)** | Protocol-level fraud monitor; challenges false peg-out assertions during the challenge window. | Yes — you can self-challenge a false assertion. |
| **Security Council** | Transitional emergency body; can pause the protocol by quorum in an incident. Does not custody BTC. | — |

"Vault Keeper" refers to the application-scoped operators (VP + AVK); the Universal Challenger is a separate protocol-level role. For the full trust model, recovery paths, and role composition, see **Safety & trust assumptions** and **Protocol actors**.
