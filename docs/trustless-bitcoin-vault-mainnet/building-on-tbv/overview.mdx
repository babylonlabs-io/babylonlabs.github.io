title: Building on TBV
---

TBV is a Bitcoin-collateral primitive for **many** applications, not just one lending market. This section is for ecosystem builders. Whatever you are building, the vault layer stays the same: BTC is locked on Bitcoin behind pre-signed spending paths, made usable on Ethereum through cryptographic proofs, and never leaves the depositor's custody.

There are three distinct ways to build on TBV. Pick the one that matches how deep you need to go.

## The two layers

TBV separates into a **vault layer** and an **application layer**.

- **Vault layer** — the protocol contracts that own the Bitcoin-collateral lifecycle: `BTCVaultRegistry` (vault state and activation), `ProtocolParams` (versioned protocol parameters), `ApplicationRegistry` (which applications may consume vaults), `CapPolicy`, `FeeEscrow`, and the metadata registry. This layer is application-agnostic. It knows nothing about lending, stablecoins, or perps — only about vaults, their lifecycle, and who is allowed to activate them.
- **Application layer** — a contract that registers with the protocol and gives the locked BTC a meaning inside a product. The first application is **Aave v4 borrowing**, implemented by `AaveAdapter` (the depositor entry point) plus its per-depositor position accounting and its internal accounting token, `vaultBTC`. Morpho Blue is the next planned application; stablecoins, perpetual DEXs, options, cross-chain markets, and staking dual-utility are on the roadmap.

Everything you build attaches to one of these two layers.

## The three builder personas

| You want to… | Build a… | You interface with | Track |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bring BTC collateral into a **new** product (a new lending market, a stablecoin, perps, options) | **New application** on the vault layer — your own adapter, your own accounting semantics, registered with `ApplicationRegistry` | Protocol contracts (`ApplicationRegistry`, `BTCVaultRegistry`) | **Application Integration** |
| Extend the **existing Aave app** — a yield, credit-delegation, or aggregation product that rides on Aave borrowing | **Resolver** — a factory of per-user position accounts on the Aave adapter | `AaveAdapter` and its position accounts | **Resolver / extensions** |
| Automate vault operations — bots, dashboards, agents, backend services | **Script or service** against the protocol and the Aave app | The Babylon TypeScript SDK | **SDK & tooling** |

## Decision guide

- **Do you want a new product on BTC collateral, with your own risk and accounting model?** Build an **application**. You register with the protocol, own your collateral semantics, and define your own liquidation and pause posture. This is the deepest integration and carries the most governance and audit responsibility. See **Application Integration**.
- **Do you want to extend Aave borrowing — for example, let a third party borrow against a depositor's collateral, or pool depositors into a shared position?** Build a **Resolver**. You ride on the existing, audited Aave application and only add the routing and authorization logic your product needs. See **Resolver overview**.
- **Do you just need programmatic vault operations** — creating vaults, activating them, borrowing, repaying, reading position health? You do not need a contract at all. Use the **SDK & tooling** track.

If you are unsure between an application and a resolver, start from the question *"do I need my own accounting token and collateral semantics?"* If yes, it is an application. If you are content to express your product as authorization rules on top of Aave positions, it is a resolver.

## What the protocol guarantees for every builder

No matter which track you choose, the vault layer gives you the same foundations:

- **Non-custody.** The depositor's Bitcoin key is required at vault creation and on every release path. No application, resolver, operator, or the protocol itself can move the BTC outside the paths pre-signed at vault creation.
- **Pre-signed spend paths.** Every legitimate Bitcoin spend — redemption after repayment, redemption after liquidation, refund, challenge responses — is constructed and signed when the vault is created. Afterwards no party can invent a new spend.
- **Trustless redemption.** BTC is released only when a zero-knowledge proof of the corresponding Ethereum state is verified on Bitcoin itself. No bridge, no wrapped token, no soft fork.
- **Fraud monitoring.** A protocol-level Universal Challenger watches for misbehaviour independently of any single application.
- **Governance-gated participation.** Applications and resolvers are accepted only after review and registration. `ApplicationRegistry` gates which applications may consume vaults; the Aave adapter accepts only resolvers an admin has registered.

What the protocol does **not** do for you is application-specific: oracle pricing, health-factor policy, liquidation economics, your accounting token's invariants, and your own upgrade and pause controls remain your responsibility. Each track below draws that line explicitly.

:::note On-chain code must be open source
Any code you deploy on-chain that extends the protocol — a new application or a resolver — must be **open source**: published in a public repository under an **OSI-approved license compatible with the protocol's license**, with its **audited code, deployed bytecode, and published source all corresponding to a single commit**. This is a registration requirement, covered in detail in the **Resolver partner checklist**.
:::

## Status

Mainnet is a capped, allowlisted **Beta**: real value, conservative limits, phased rollout. At Beta the only live application is Aave v4, and new-application onboarding is handled directly with the TBV team rather than through open self-service registration. Numeric limits are announced at launch.
